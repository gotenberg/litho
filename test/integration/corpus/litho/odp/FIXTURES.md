# litho/odp corpus fixtures

OpenDocument presentations that lock how a conversion applies the paper, the orientation and the notes pages to a deck, scored by the `diff` feature (see `FEATURES.md`) against LibreOffice Impress. No corpus of LibreOffice's test documents carries the route's options.

Each source is a directory, `sources/<name>/`, holding one flat OpenDocument file, plain XML that lives in git, and optionally `options.json`, the LibreOffice route's form fields it converts with (`fixture.LoadOffice` reads them as for the other Office corpora: `paperWidth` and `paperHeight` in inches, `landscape`, `nativePages`, `nativeBookmarks`, `exportNotesPages`). A source that differs from another by its options alone links that source's document. The references are LibreOffice's, built by `corpus references` with the image `GOTENBERG_IMAGE` in `corpus/versions.env` pins (`gotenberg/gotenberg:9.4.1`, LibreOffice 26.8.2.2) through `/forms/libreoffice/convert`, into the testdata, never committed. The documents draw Liberation Serif and Liberation Sans, which the image and the fixture fonts both carry, and were written by a script for this corpus.

Impress prints no handout from the command line or through its PDF export, so no reference engine prints `handouts-deck`'s handouts. Each `handouts-N` option fixture names `handouts-N-ref` as its `reference_of`: a document whose plain conversion is the expected handout pages, whose LibreOffice reference the `diff` cell scores litho's handouts against. Each page of a `-ref` document takes the name of its first slide, so the bookmark LibreOffice writes for it is one litho writes, leading to the same page. The `diff` cell so scores how litho draws a handout page, not where it places the slides: the `-ref` documents take their rectangles from litho's rule. That rule is locked to LibreOffice by `TestHandoutAreas_Impress` in `pkg/internal/convert/slides`: the 48 layouts the pinned LibreOffice stores in the handout master, for every count of slides a page. Where the master's texts go on a turned page is litho's own rule, the one LibreOffice sizes the boxes of its own handout master by (`TestTurnBox`): LibreOffice turns a handout page in its print dialog alone.

## Writing systems

The presentations below each lock how Impress prints one script in text boxes. Their running text is the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in each language, the United Nations' translations as the UDHR in Unicode project transcribes them (`efele.net/udhr`, `github.com/eric-muller/udhr`); the strings that show a rule come from the standard that states it, cited in the document. Each presentation names its faces in its paragraph styles, and a list its bullet's face, faces of the Gotenberg image that the fixture fonts carry: Liberation Sans for Latin, Noto Sans CJK JP, SC, TC and KR as the Asian font, and DejaVu Sans (Arabic, Hebrew), Noto Sans Devanagari or Noto Sans Thai as the complex font, each with its `style:language-*` and `style:country-*`. The faces in the table are those `pdffonts` reads in each reference. Asian text stays at the regular weight: the fixture fonts hold no bold Noto Sans CJK, which the image has.

Measured on the references: Impress sets a text box vertical when the paragraph properties of its graphic style name `style:writing-mode="tb-rl"`, not when its graphic properties alone do; it adds space between Japanese and digits or Latin letters. With the Asian and complex fonts named only in the default graphic style, Impress draws Japanese in Noto Sans CJK SC and the spaces and digits of an Arabic line in Noto Sans Devanagari, and a bullet without a face in Noto Sans: these presentations name every face in their paragraph and list styles.

## The caller's paper

Impress applies neither the route's paper nor its landscape to a presentation: it prints each slide on a page of the slide size. litho follows its own rule, the same in every format: the caller's paper is every page, and a slide is scaled down to fit it, never up, and centred. No print of Impress's shows that rule on `wide-deck-paper` and `portrait-deck-landscape`, so each names a twin as its `reference_of`: `wide-deck-paper-ref` and `portrait-deck-landscape-ref`, decks whose slides are the paper and whose frames are the source's, scaled by the fit and moved to where the centred slide lies. The `diff` cell so scores the fitted slide against Impress's print of the same page, and the `targets` cell converts the two fixtures at their options to every output flavour.

A twin states what Impress draws of its source, measured on the source's reference: the text at 18 pt, whatever size the default graphic style names, 0.222 cm inside the frame's sides and 0.0984 cm below its top. It carries the scaled size in a paragraph style, since Impress takes a text size from a paragraph style and not from a graphic one, and the scaled insets as the frame's padding. The rule itself is locked by the public API tests (`TestConvert_SlidesFitThePaper` in `pkg/pdf/convert_pagesetup_test.go`, which scales a wide slide to Letter and turns a portrait one).
