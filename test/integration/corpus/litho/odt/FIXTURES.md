# litho/odt corpus fixtures

OpenDocument text documents that lock how a conversion applies the paper and the orientation to the sections of a document, prints tracked changes and fills date fields, scored by the `diff` feature (see `FEATURES.md`) against LibreOffice. No corpus of LibreOffice's test documents carries the route's options.

Each source is a directory, `sources/<name>/`, holding one flat OpenDocument file, plain XML that lives in git, and optionally `options.json`, the LibreOffice route's form fields it converts with (`fixture.LoadOffice` reads them as for the other Office corpora: `paperWidth` and `paperHeight` in inches, `landscape`, `nativePages`, `nativeBookmarks`, `exportNotesPages`, `exportFormFields`), and litho's own `trackedChanges` and `date`, which no LibreOffice route field carries: a source that sets one names under `reference_of` a twin whose plain conversion is the expected result, and is judged against the twin's reference. A source that differs from another by its options alone links that source's document. The references are LibreOffice's, built by `corpus references` with the image `GOTENBERG_IMAGE` in `corpus/versions.env` pins (`gotenberg/gotenberg:9.4.1`, LibreOffice 26.8.2.2) through `/forms/libreoffice/convert`, into the testdata, never committed. The documents draw Liberation Serif and Liberation Sans, which the image and the fixture fonts both carry, and were written by a script for this corpus.

The pinned image leaves LibreOffice's locale empty, so LibreOffice prints a page that names no size on A4 (`no-page-size`: 595.3 by 841.9 points), litho's default too: the `diff` feature converts these sources with no default paper.

## Writing systems

The documents below each lock how LibreOffice sets one script. Their running text is the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in each language, the United Nations' translations as the UDHR in Unicode project transcribes them (`efele.net/udhr`, `github.com/eric-muller/udhr`); the strings that show a rule come from the standard that states it, cited in the document. Each document names its faces in its styles, faces of the Gotenberg image that the fixture fonts carry: Liberation Sans for Latin, Noto Sans CJK JP, SC, TC and KR as the Asian font, and DejaVu Sans (Arabic, Hebrew), Noto Sans Devanagari or Noto Sans Thai as the complex font, each with its `style:language-*` and `style:country-*`. The faces in the table are those `pdffonts` reads in each reference. Asian text stays at the regular weight: the fixture fonts hold no bold Noto Sans CJK, which the image has.

| ------------------ | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `ja-line-break`    | 3                   | Noto Sans CJK JP, Liberation Sans                | The fourteen strings of `litho/html`'s `ja-line-break`, each in a paragraph whose right indent leaves a column as wide as the characters before the marked one, three times: forbidden characters applied (`style:line-break="strict"`), not applied (`normal`), and applied with `style:punctuation-wrap="hanging"` |
| `cjk-han-variants` | 1                   | Noto Sans CJK JP, SC, TC and KR, Liberation Sans | Eleven ideographs whose regional forms differ at 22 points, in Noto Sans CJK JP under the Asian languages ja-JP, zh-CN, zh-TW and ko-KR, then in each language's face, and article 1 in each language; the sizes are `style:font-size-asian` alone                                                                   |

Measured on the references: with `style:line-break="normal"` LibreOffice starts a line with a comma or a full stop, and with `hanging` it hangs them past the right indent; with `strict` it does not break before a small kana, which `litho/html`'s Chromium does under `normal`. In a right-to-left paragraph, `start` aligns right, `end` left, `fo:margin-left` indents the right side, and `justify` stretches the Arabic words (kashida) rather than the spaces. The table's columns run right to left. The Korean column breaks at spaces only, never between two syllables of a word.

## Expected text

Five sources print text LibreOffice writes in a form PDF.js reads apart from the page. Each names `expectedText` in its `options.json`, beside a file `expected-text.txt` holding the page's text in reading order, written from the source's runs: what the pages print, generated list numbers and bullets included, the characters as the source holds them. A line ends where the reference's page ends it inside Thai or CJK text, which no space parts, so that a reader's tokens are the file's (`FEATURES.md`, "Expected text"). The `diff` cell scores litho's text against the file and reads the reference for the pages, layout, links and outline alone; neither the key nor the file changes the reference. What the reference holds, as PDF.js reads it, and what the file adds:

| Source            | What PDF.js reads of the reference                                                                                                             | The file                                                                               |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `ar-rtl`          | Lines in visual order, lam-alef split (`اإلعالن`), marks as separate items and `3` for a missing mapping (`التمت3ع`), digits glued (`المادة1`) | The paragraphs in logical order, the list's `1.` to `3.`, the table's cells row by row |
| `he-bidi-numbers` | The lines that mix scripts in visual order, spaces around numbers dropped (`ב־10בדצמבר1948בפריז`)                                              | The paragraphs, the list's `1.` to `3.`, the cells                                     |
| `hi-devanagari`   | Vowel signs and parts of conjuncts as items of their own (`मनु ष्यों`), signs mapped to other characters                                       | The paragraphs and the R2 to R16 strings                                               |
| `th-line-break`   | Tone and vowel marks as items of their own or mapped to other characters (`ทั้  งหลาย์`)                                                       | The paragraphs, each Thai line ended where the page ends it                            |
| `ja-vertical`     | One character a line: PDF.js reads a vertical column as items one glyph tall                                                                   | The paragraphs, each column a line as the page breaks them                             |

poppler reads `hi-devanagari` and `th-line-break` from the references as the source states them, so the ToUnicode maps hold the text and PDF.js's items part it; it reads `ar-rtl` with the stray `3`s too.

## Paper documents

`corpus build` generates the paper documents from the committed `paper-base` into the testdata (`paperDocs` in `cmd/corpus/papers.go`, `papers/<name>/`), never committed: the same base gives the same bytes. `paper-base` is the text of `litho/docx`'s `paper-base` in ODF: about three pages on portrait Letter (page layout `pm1`), Cambria headings, Calibri text, a Times New Roman quote, an Arial table, an inline picture, a header and a "Page X of Y" footer. The LibreOffice route takes the paper options, so the option documents have references of their own. Measured on the references: LibreOffice prints each option document as it prints the document stating that paper itself, the same pages and the same text (`paper-option-a5-landscape` and `paper-a5-landscape`: six 595.3 by 419.6 point pages).

| Fixture                                     | Count | Page setup                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `paper-base`                                | 1     | Committed, portrait Letter                                                                                                                                      |
| `paper-<paper>-<orientation>`               | 16    | `pm1` on A3, A4, A5 and B5 (ISO 216), Letter, Legal, Tabloid and Executive (`fo:page-width`, `fo:page-height`, `style:print-orientation`), in both orientations |
| `paper-option-<paper>-<orientation>`        | 16    | `paper-base` with `paperWidth` and `paperHeight` of each of the eight papers, and `landscape` for the landscape ones                                            |
| `paper-mixed-orientation`                   | 1     | The second Heading 1 starts the master page `Second`: portrait Letter, then landscape Letter                                                                    |
| `paper-mixed-sizes`                         | 1     | Portrait A4, then portrait A5                                                                                                                                   |
| `paper-default-<orientation>`               | 2     | `pm1` without `fo:page-width` and `fo:page-height`, portrait or landscape: LibreOffice's default paper, A4                                                      |
| `paper-margins-<set>-<paper>-<orientation>` | 18    | `pm1` on A5, A4 or A3 in both orientations, with the `fo:margin-*` of a margin set (below)                                                                      |

The margin sets, in inches, are those of `litho/docx`: `none`, all 0; `narrow`, 0.5 on every side; `uneven`, top 1, right 0.25, bottom 0.75, left 0.5. An OpenDocument header lies inside the page margins, its height and spacing taken from the body (ODF 1.3 Part 3, 17.4), so the sets' header and footer distances have no counterpart and the margin documents keep the base's header and footer styles. They lie on A5, A4, LibreOffice's default, and A3.
